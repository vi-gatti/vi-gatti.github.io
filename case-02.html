01

Overview & challenge

Strategic and UX research for a major Brazilian telecommunications provider, as part of a digital transformation that brought its residential and mobile self-service apps into a single experience.

Residential and mobile customers managed their services in separate apps, even when they were clients of both. Fragmentation was also internal: different systems and teams made it hard to build a single view of the customer.

Customers should not need to understand how the company is organised in order to solve their problems.

The research contributed to bringing two separate self-service apps into one experience, from discovery to the first phase of implementation.

02

Research strategy

Questions the research had to answer:

  1. How do residential, mobile and combined customers use their services?
  2. Where do current digital journeys create friction?
  3. How do competitors organise their digital services?
  4. Which customers would the integration affect most?
  5. Do existing and proposed features work for users?

A mixed-methods approach in three moments, run alongside the integration work.

  1. 01

    Research and discovery

    Customer and stakeholder interviews, questionnaires, NPS and TNPS analysis, market and competitor research and journey analysis. The results were synthesised into three research-based personas.

  2. 02

    Strategic alignment

    Findings were discussed with UX and design, product, technology and leadership, connecting customer evidence to the priorities of the integration.

  3. 03

    Implementation follow-up

    Moderated and unmoderated usability tests continued as new flows were proposed, through the first integration deliveries.

Research in practice

04

Key insights & design directions

Residential and mobile customers showed distinct patterns of behaviour and satisfaction. Three personas made these differences visible to the teams, including customers with combined services.

Above all, integration had to go beyond consolidating features. Each part of the experience the research examined became a direction discussed with the teams.

  1. Part of the experience examined

    Design direction

  2. Part of the experience examined

    Access: customers with both services used two separate apps.

    Design direction

    Unified access to residential and mobile services.

  3. Part of the experience examined

    Product management, split by service.

    Design direction

    More centralised management of products.

  4. Part of the experience examined

    Contracts and billing: customers needed more visibility of the services they had subscribed to and of their billing information.

    Design direction

    More transparent contracts and clearer access to billing information.

  5. Part of the experience examined

    Moving between self-service and human support.

    Design direction

    Integrated service channels, so staff can access the customer’s information and history and resolve issues more easily.

The market and competitive analysis placed these findings within the wider digital service landscape.

05

Transformation impact

The directions above informed the integration of the two self-service apps into one, simplifying how customers accessed and managed residential and mobile services. I stayed involved until the first integration deliveries, within a wider transformation that continued after the project.

06

My contribution

I worked as the UX Researcher alongside the UX and design manager, on the research and alignment of the integration.

I conducted customer and stakeholder research, analysed journeys across residential and mobile services, developed the personas, ran the usability tests and brought the evidence into discussions with product, design and technology. I also took part in the weekly cross-functional follow-up as the first phase of the integration moved into delivery.
