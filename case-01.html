Product Discovery & Go‑to‑Market Assessment

Assessing the market opportunity and business feasibility of an insurance product linked to B2B corporate cards.

01

Project overview

A strategic discovery for a major Brazilian financial institution, assessing whether to launch a paid insurance product linked to its B2B corporate cards.

The study combined market research, international benchmarking, internal data and stakeholder interviews to assess demand, value proposition and business potential.

The assessment supported the decision to pause the initiative before the initial investment, avoiding an estimated US$200,000 in upfront costs.

Industry
Financial services and insurance
Organisation
Major Brazilian financial institution
Project duration
13 weeks
Year
2025

02

Context

The institution was looking for new revenue in its corporate card portfolio. One option was an insurance product offered on top of the cards, for an additional fee.

Understanding the opportunity meant looking beyond the product idea: the competitive landscape, existing insurance models, distribution, and the card product itself, which was going through technical and team restructuring.

03

The challenge

There was little evidence that companies would pay for an additional insurance product. The work also had to align several teams in a complex organisation and turn the findings into a clear narrative for executive decisions.

Questions the research had to answer:

  1. Was there a market for this proposition?
  2. What alternatives did corporate card customers already have?
  3. How were similar products positioned and distributed abroad?
  4. Was the expected return worth the investment and effort?

The challenge was to assess the opportunity critically, including the possibility that the market or internal dependencies did not support an immediate launch.

04

Research objectives

The research aimed to:

  1. Map the Brazilian market and identify direct and indirect competitors.

  2. Extend the benchmarking to international markets when local references proved insufficient.

  3. Compare value propositions, positioning and distribution models.

  4. Understand the maturity, channels and experience of the corporate card product.

  5. Evaluate business scenarios, implementation requirements and dependencies.

05

Research approach

A mixed-methods discovery in two waves, followed by decision support.

  1. 01

    Exploratory wave

    Brazilian market research and competitive analysis, international benchmarking across the United States, Europe and Asia, stakeholder interviews and analysis of internal business data.

    With no direct equivalent in Brazil, the benchmarking moved abroad: the absence of local competitors was treated as a question, not as evidence of an opportunity.

  2. 02

    Structuring wave

    Journey mapping, usage analysis, market sizing, value proposition scenarios, cost and revenue analysis and technology discovery.

  3. 03

    Decision support

    Findings were synthesised into a narrative for executive decisions, and timing was reassessed as the restructuring of the card product and its satisfaction scores became clearer.

06

Key findings

  1. No direct equivalent existed in Brazil for a paid, card-linked insurance product.

    The closest alternatives were insurance benefits already included in some cards.

  2. International benchmarking found comparable B2B propositions abroad.

    They showed different value propositions and distribution models, but did not prove the model would work in Brazil.

  3. The card product itself had experience gaps.

    The digital channel offered a single card type, with restrictions, and was rarely used. Contracting and card delivery were the main pain points in customer feedback.

  4. Implementation required significant effort and investment.

    Coordination across areas, an upfront investment and a longer path to profitability than the business expected.

Customer voices

  • The corporate cards offered do not provide benefits for the company profile.
  • It is always the customer who needs to fill out, register and do everything alone, even if it means wasting a lot of time on multiple attempts.
  • The mandatory token ends up preventing some purchases and causing delays in the operation.

07

From research to decisions

Findings were discussed with business and other stakeholders to look beyond the initial commercial appeal.

Dimensions considered together:

  1. Competitive landscape and market opportunity.
  2. Relevance of the value proposition.
  3. Commercial model and expected return.
  4. Maturity and timing of the card product.
  5. Technical and organisational conditions.

This broader view challenged the assumption that an interesting market opportunity was enough to justify an immediate launch.

08

Outcomes

The assessment contributed to the decision to pause the project before the initial investment, while key technical and strategic dependencies were still unresolved.

My contribution

My work included Brazilian and international benchmarking, analysis of propositions and distribution models, internal data analysis, synthesis of findings and participation in the strategic discussions on feasibility.
